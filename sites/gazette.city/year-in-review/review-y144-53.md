# Year in review — Y144 to Y153

**Y144** — The council notice board is replaced; notices pinned by date, oldest at the bottom. Swiftline applies to build a 2x2 warehouse beside the belt yard. Ben Marsh writes about bearings that run hot.

**Y146** — The council mower is lent to the school field; the grass by the weigh house is long. Wellbourne applies to run a pipe under Mill Lane to the new pump house. Cricket season opens.

**Y148** — The weighing house scales work again, tested with a sack of coal and a brick. Cinderhall applies to fire a second blast furnace. Ira Bell still has the rope; Ted Vok still wants it.

**Y149** — Six street lamps on Mill Lane are converted to new fittings; the old lamps go to the shed. Pillar & Sons applies to roof the girder rack. The mill league pauses for snow.

**Y150** — Wellbourne lays pipe to the pump house on Mill Lane; water goes wherever the pipes do. Underhill sells two kilns to the city yards. One needle is used at a notice day, and it is not for tea.

**Y151** — Halverson applies to keep a larger silicon scrap pile behind the arc furnace yard. Clem Ortiz dies; he read the Gazette every morning at the library. Brannock beats Swiftline in a replay after the floodlight fails.

**Y152** — The Gearwright crude assembler builds its thousandth gearbox and goes straight to one thousand and one. Moll Peck's mangle ad remains on the board; she asked for no replies and none are given. Ben Rye's soup becomes a potluck fixture.

**Y153** — Pillar applies for a reinforced concrete floor in the press shop extension. Nessa Cragg dies; her carbon copies fill a drawer at the council office. The school roof leaks in the north corridor.

The decade closes with the Gazette still monthly, the mangle file closed at ten sheets, and the teapot still unclaimed after thirty years.
