# Year in review — Y124 to Y133

The founding decade of the Municipal Gazette. The first issue appeared Y124-11-03, edited by Tessa Roe, with Harold Pimm as council clerk on Mill Lane. The paper cost a penny and came out each month.

**Y124** — The council office opens on Mill Lane, open Tuesdays and Thursdays. Six new street lamps are lit on Mill Lane in Y126, bright enough, Old Man Hargrave says, to read the Gazette by. Moll Peck places her first mangle ad in Y124; it runs, unanswered, until Y131.

**Y128** — Mill Lane is resurfaced, or the notice says so. Gravel is ordered from the rail siding. Cinderhall Foundry applies to extend its foundry shed over the coke oven yard. Ben Marsh writes about plate that warps.

**Y130** — Underhill Excavation applies to build a kiln firing eight at a time, burning coal: stone into brick, sand into glass. The council gets a new desk, oak, second-hand from the trade post, four crowns. Ned Cragg dies; Nessa Cragg keeps his carbon copies.

**Y132** — Harold Pimm starts as council clerk. Gearwright Machine Works applies to build the crude assembler shed beside the foundry yard: four by four, ten seconds a part. Old Man Hargrave dies; the Tuesday seat at the library is empty. The mill league is formed, teams from each mill.

**Y133** — The Gazette finds its shape: council notices, planning applications, crossword, obituaries, letters, sport, small ads. The teapot prize is offered for the first time and is never claimed.

By Y133 the cast is set: Tessa Roe editing, Harold Pimm filing, Rita Sloane setting crosswords, Jem Croft covering sport, Moll Peck advertising her mangle, Ben Marsh writing about screws. The mangle file, as the editor later counts it, will grow to ten sheets.
