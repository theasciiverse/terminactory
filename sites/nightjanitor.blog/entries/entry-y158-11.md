# Y158-11-02

The furnaces are cold and the floor is empty. I walked the length of it twice and met no one. First round at half eight, after the night shift starts. Second round at half ten, when the break should be over. Third round just before six, before the gates open. The lights stay on all night; I do not switch them off, and no one switches them on.

The bins by the back gate are full of nothing. I looked. The ash silo is full again, Cinderhall keeps feeding it, and I keep walking past it. The gate at the yard was locked; I have the key from Simon Kyrk's pocket, or so the story goes. I never found where he put the others. My own keys fit the same doors, or so I assume. I have not tried them all.

The tea trolley does not come at night. Mrs Gable finishes at six and takes no slice herself. I brought my own: a thermos of tea, two slices of bread, cold. I ate it by the cold boiler, where the pipes hum without steam. The boiler shop break is too short to reach the trolley and back, Rupert Hale says, and I believe him. I do not take breaks; I take rounds.

Everything is where I left it. That is the job. I locked the gate behind me and the key turned the first time.
