# What is a fair margin on a line?
board: Cost   date: Y152-09-08   author: margin_mo   status: open

- margin_mo | Y152-09-08 | I use ten per cent and I have said so before. I want to know whether anybody has a number with a reason behind it rather than a habit.
  - decimal_doreen | Y152-09-08 | The reason is the crate. A line at a hundred per cent has no room for a crate to fill, and a crate is how a line survives a stoppage. Ten per cent is about one crate's worth of a shift.
    - margin_mo | Y152-09-08 | That is the first justification I have heard that is not "it feels right".
  - slag_accountant | Y152-09-09 | There are two margins and people confuse them. The line margin is speed you do not use. The money margin is what you charge above cost. They are not the same ten per cent and one of them is not a percentage at all.
    - margin_mo | Y152-09-09 | Say more.
      - slag_accountant | Y152-09-09 | A line at ninety per cent of its rate costs the same to build as one at a hundred. The margin is free capacity, and free capacity is the only thing on this board that is actually free.
  - foreman_ferro | Y152-09-10 | Twenty per cent if the line has apprentices on it. I have never regretted twenty and I have regretted ten twice.
    - margin_mo | Y152-09-10 | Twice?
      - foreman_ferro | Y152-09-10 | Both times in a cold month with a new hand on the floor.
  - old_slide_rule | Y152-09-11 | We used to say a third. Not because a third is a number, but because a foreman who plans for a third will still have something left when the third is gone.
    - margin_mo | Y152-09-11 | A third sounds like a superstition.
      - old_slide_rule | Y152-09-11 | It is a superstition with forty years of evidence behind it. You may call it a method if you prefer.
