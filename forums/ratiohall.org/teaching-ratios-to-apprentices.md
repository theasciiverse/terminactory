# How do you teach a ratio to somebody who has never built a line?
board: Method   date: Y154-01-09   author: unit_ursula   status: open

- unit_ursula | Y154-01-09 | I have four apprentices and a blackboard. Last year I taught them the table first and they could recite it and could not build anything. This year I am doing it the other way and I would like to know what works where you are.
  - foreman_ferro | Y154-01-09 | Build first. Give them a furnace, a molder and a belt and let them find out that one molder serves two furnaces by watching the second one wait. Then show the table and watch them recognise it.
    - unit_ursula | Y154-01-09 | How long does that take?
      - foreman_ferro | Y154-01-09 | An afternoon. The table takes ten minutes and is forgotten by Friday.
  - decimal_doreen | Y154-01-10 | Teach the unit before the number. Per minute, per second, per machine. Half the confusion on this board is people comparing a per-minute figure with a per-second one and never noticing.
    - unit_ursula | Y154-01-10 | That is going on the board in letters a foot high.
  - table_herr | Y154-01-10 | Draw the arrows. A line of arrows with a machine at each one and a number between them is a ratio an apprentice can carry in his head. A table is a ratio he can carry in his pocket and never open.
    - unit_ursula | Y154-01-11 | Do you draw for them or make them draw?
      - table_herr | Y154-01-11 | Make them draw. A drawing you copied is a drawing you do not understand.
  - half_belt_hana | Y154-01-11 | Give them a line that is wrong. Ours had a press feeding a press with nothing between, and the apprentices found the starvation in an hour and argued about it for a day.
    - unit_ursula | Y154-01-12 | What did they conclude?
      - half_belt_hana | Y154-01-12 | That the first press was idle and the second was starving, and that both were true, and that a crate was the answer. They will not forget it.
  - pinch_point | Y154-01-13 | I learned from a man who made me count a shift before he would tell me anything. I hated it. I have used it every year since.
    - unit_ursula | Y154-01-13 | Then I will make them count a shift.
  - brass_tally | Y154-01-20 | One thing I would add, from the other end. Tell them which numbers are not worth knowing. A molder at twenty-four a minute against a furnace at twelve is worth knowing. The third decimal of a belt is not, and an apprentice who learns to chase it will build nothing.
    - unit_ursula | Y154-01-20 | That is the hardest lesson on this board and you have put it in two sentences.
