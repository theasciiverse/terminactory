board: Throughput   date: Y145-05-20   author: pinch_point   status: open

- pinch_point | Y145-05-20 | I keep being asked this and the answer is "it depends", which is not an answer. Can we put a number on it for each drill?
  - table_herr | Y145-05-20 | The numbers are not on the board and I will not invent them. What I can give you is the method. Count what a drill delivers in ten minutes on your own floor, because the patch matters and the belt matters.
    - pinch_point | Y145-05-20 | That is the honest answer and it is not the one people want.
      - table_herr | Y145-05-20 | The dishonesty would be quicker.
  - six_belts | Y145-05-21 | A cheap drill will not keep a furnace busy. A basic drill will. Two basic drills on one furnace is a queue, not a fault.
    - pinch_point | Y145-05-21 | A queue is a fault if you paid for the second drill.
      - six_belts | Y145-05-21 | Then do not pay for it.
  - foreman_ferro | Y145-05-21 | And a drill burns coal whether it is delivering or not. A queue of drills is a queue of fires.
    - pinch_point | Y145-05-22 | That is the part I always forget.
  - half_belt_hana | Y145-06-02 | We counted: one basic drill and one furnace was enough for us, and the second drill went to a second furnace. Two furnaces, two drills, one molder between them taking turns. It has run for three years.
    - pinch_point | Y145-06-02 | That is a better answer than mine and it has a date on it.
  - decimal_doreen | Y145-06-03 | For the file: a furnace melts twelve copper a minute as whole ore. A basic drill is faster than that or it would not be called basic. The useful question is not how many drills per furnace, it is how many furnaces you can feed from the patch you have.
