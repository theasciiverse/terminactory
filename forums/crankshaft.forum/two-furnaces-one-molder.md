# Can two furnaces share one molder?
board: Beginner Questions   date: Y135-03-22   author: molder_max   status: solved

- molder_max | Y135-03-22 | Asked and answered a hundred times, but here it is with the numbers. I run a molder behind two copper furnaces. The furnaces each pour every five seconds. The molder casts every two and a half. So the molder can keep up with both if they take turns, and it does, and nothing jams.
  What it cannot do is keep up with both and also with a third. The third furnace will sit with its metal waiting and its block will say FULL, and you will think the furnace is broken.
  - molder_max | Y135-03-22 | Edit: solved in my own head before I posted. But if anyone has a different arrangement I would like to hear it.
  - brass_tally | Y135-03-22 | Your arithmetic is right. The thing to watch is the pipe. Two furnaces into one pipe run is fine for one metal, but if the run also carries anything else you have a second problem.
    - molder_max | Y135-03-22 | No pipe. Both furnaces face the molder, one on each side.
      - brass_tally | Y135-03-23 | Then you have the tidiest version of it. Nothing to fix.
  - pipefitter_uri | Y135-03-23 | A molder takes molten metal from a pipe on any side but its front, and from a furnace that faces it. Two furnaces facing it from two sides is allowed. I have seen it and it works.
    - grey_belt | Y135-03-23 | Does the molder get confused about which metal?
      - pipefitter_uri | Y135-03-24 | A molder has a recipe, like any machine. It casts what its recipe says. If you point a copper furnace and an iron furnace at the same molder, you will get whichever metal arrives and a great deal of confusion. One metal, one molder.
        - molder_max | Y135-03-24 | One metal, one molder. I have it carved on the machine.
