# One pump for two boilers: enough water?
board: Power   date: Y139-08-12   author: slow_steam   status: open

- slow_steam | Y139-08-12 | Is one pump enough for two boilers? Each boiler takes ten water a second at full steam and the pump gives twenty.
  - ivo_p | Y139-08-12 | On paper, yes. On a shared run, no, because the two boilers are not drinking at the same rate at the same moment, and the pipe run holds what it holds. You will see one boiler starve while the other is full.
    - slow_steam | Y139-08-12 | Even at exactly twenty?
      - ivo_p | Y139-08-12 | Especially at exactly twenty. There is no margin in the pipe for a busy minute.
  - kettle_ron | Y139-08-13 | I ran two boilers on one pump for six years and it was fine. Then we added a third machine to the same run and it was not fine. The pump was never the problem until it was.
    - slow_steam | Y139-08-13 | That is not reassuring.
      - kettle_ron | Y139-08-13 | It is not meant to be. It is meant to be true.
  - pipefitter_uri | Y139-08-14 | The advanced pump gives four times the water and it is two by two. If you are going to want a third boiler in five years, put the advanced pump in now and run one pipe.
    - slow_steam | Y139-08-14 | We are going to want a third boiler in two years.
      - pipefitter_uri | Y139-08-14 | Then you have your answer and it is not the cheap one.
  - decimal_doreen | Y139-08-15 | For the file: two boilers at full steam are twenty water a second and one pump is twenty water a second. A run at exactly its supply is a run that is already behind.
