# Girders: press or rolling mill?
board: Build Orders & Layouts   date: Y144-02-19   author: tiny_lathe   status: open

- tiny_lathe | Y144-02-19 | I make girders in a press, two steel for one. The rolling mill says three steel for two girders, which is one and a half a girder. That is better. But the mill also makes sheets and the press makes other things. Which do you keep on girders?
  - ferrous_ann | Y144-02-19 | The mill, if girders are the job. Three steel for two is the better bargain and the mill is faster at it. Keep the press for plate, pipe and gears.
  - molder_max | Y144-02-20 | I keep both and run girders on whichever is idle. That is not an answer, it is a confession.
    - tiny_lathe | Y144-02-20 | It is an honest one.
  - halvard_k | Y144-02-20 | Hello. I have run the press for girders for years because the mill was busy on sheets. The press is slower but it is always there.
    - ferrous_ann | Y144-02-20 | "Always there" beats "better on paper" more often than the numbers men admit.
      - throughput_tony | Y144-02-21 | It does not beat it. It beats it only when the mill is genuinely occupied. If the mill is idle and the press is making girders, you are paying a third more steel for nothing.
        - halvard_k | Y144-02-21 | Then I will move girders to the mill when the sheets are done.
          - throughput_tony | Y144-02-21 | That is the correct answer and it took four posts.
  - second_floor_ivy | Y144-02-22 | A layout note. Girders are heavy and they go into frames and beams. Put the mill near the welder if you can. Frames take two girders and eight screws, and a welder waiting on girders is a frame line that stops.
    - tiny_lathe | Y144-02-22 | And screws come from the lathe, so the welder wants the lathe nearby too. I have three machines that all want to be in the same corner.
      - second_floor_ivy | Y144-02-23 | That corner is the busiest corner in the plant. Everybody builds it twice.
  - rivet_pete | Y144-02-25 | A girder that has been pressed is a girder with a stamp on it. A girder that has been rolled is a girder. I fit both and I can tell you which one I would rather have in a frame.
    - tiny_lathe | Y144-02-25 | The rolled one.
      - rivet_pete | Y144-02-25 | The rolled one.

  - GerdV | Y144-02-25 | From the welder's side. A girder that is a little short is scrap and a girder that is a little long is a lesson. The mill's girders are more consistent in my experience, though I could not tell you why, and I would not want to argue it with a numbers man.
    - brass_tally | Y144-02-25 | You will not have to. It is the mill's rollers. They squeeze, they do not stamp.
      - GerdV | Y144-02-25 | There it is. Thank you.
  - ratio_warden | Y144-03-01 | For the record, the mill makes two girders from three steel, which is one and a half steel per girder. The press makes one girder from two steel, which is two. The mill is better by a quarter. Anyone who says "much better" is wrong by a small amount, which is the worst kind of wrong.
    - tiny_lathe | Y144-03-01 | Noted and accepted.
