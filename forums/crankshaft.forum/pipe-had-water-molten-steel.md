# Molten steel won't go into a pipe that had water in it
board: Troubleshooting   date: Y146-05-16   author: pipefitter_uri   status: solved

- pipefitter_uri | Y146-05-16 | Not a question. A reminder, because I have just spent an hour on it and I am supposed to know better. I ran a pipe from a blast furnace to a molder. The pipe had been the water line for the boiler. The steel would not go in. The furnace stopped with a clear front and a clear back and the pipe looked empty.
  A joined run of pipe is one pool and it holds one fluid at a time. Water was still in it. I drained it by taking the pump off and letting the mixer use it up, and then the steel went in.
  - kettle_ron | Y146-05-16 | You are not the first and you will not be the last. The pipe does not tell you what is in it.

  - ivo_p | Y146-05-17 | The safe habit is one run, one fluid, for the life of the run. If you must change what a run carries, drain it first and then run the new fluid through a machine that can take it before you trust it.
    - pipefitter_uri | Y146-05-17 | That is the habit I have just re-learned.
  - brass_tally | Y146-05-17 | And the furnace's front was clear because the pipe was not a molder. A pipe that refuses a fluid is a pipe with no room in it, and the furnace cannot tell the difference between a blocked pipe and a full one.
    - pipefitter_uri | Y146-05-17 | That is exactly the trap. Thank you.
