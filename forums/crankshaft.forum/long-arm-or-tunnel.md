# Long arm or belt tunnel for crossing a line?
board: Build Orders & Layouts   date: Y144-07-19   author: mateo_belts   status: solved

- mateo_belts | Y144-07-19 | I need to get a coal belt across a machine block. I have both researches. Long arm or tunnel?
  - six_belts | Y144-07-19 | Tunnel. A belt tunnel carries a full belt under four tiles and does not care what is on top. A long arm carries one item at a time and takes power.
    - mateo_belts | Y144-07-19 | The tunnel needs an exit.
      - six_belts | Y144-07-19 | The tunnel needs an exit and the exit must face the same way as the entrance. Build the exit first, then the entrance. If you build the entrance first and walk away, it waits and looks broken.
        - mateo_belts | Y144-07-19 | That is exactly what I would have done.
          - six_belts | Y144-07-20 | Everybody does. The trick is to build the far end first.
  - pipefitter_uri | Y144-07-20 | A long arm is for a lift, not a crossing. It grabs two behind and drops two ahead, over whatever is between. If you need to move one item over one obstacle and the belt can stay where it is, the arm is tidier. If you need volume, the tunnel.
    - mateo_belts | Y144-07-20 | It is coal for a boiler. Volume.
      - pipefitter_uri | Y144-07-20 | Tunnel.
  - ivo_p | Y144-07-21 | And the arm draws power. A long arm takes more than the short one. A row of them over a busy line is a row of small loads on the grid. A tunnel takes nothing.
    - six_belts | Y144-07-21 | Tunnels do not draw EC. Belts do, a little. Worth knowing when the grid is tight.
  - crated_up | Y144-07-21 | If you are crossing more than four tiles, neither. Go round. A long detour is cheaper than a second tunnel and easier to see.
    - mateo_belts | Y144-07-22 | It is three tiles. Tunnel it is. Edit: solved, and the exit is on the far side facing the right way.
