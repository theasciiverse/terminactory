# When do you hold a price and when do you let it move?
board: Rates   date: Y156-10-11   author: copperkate   status: open

- copperkate | Y156-10-11 | I have held the board's rate through four changes in the price list and I have been wrong about the timing twice. I would like to hear how the rest of you decide.
  - old_ferrous | Y156-10-11 | Hold through a shortage and move after it. A price that rises in a shortage is a price your customers remember in the quiet season.
    - copperkate | Y156-10-11 | And a price that does not rise in a shortage is a price that empties your store.
      - old_ferrous | Y156-10-11 | Then empty the store and keep the customer. That is what a store is for.
  - slag_accountant | Y156-10-12 | Move when the cost moves and say so on the same day. A price change that arrives with an explanation is a price. One that arrives without is a rumour.
    - copperkate | Y156-10-12 | I have done both. The explained ones cost me less.
  - barter_bert | Y156-10-12 | I never hold a price. I hold a handshake and the price is whatever we say at the gate. It has worked for thirty years and it has cost me a fortune twice.
    - copperkate | Y156-10-12 | Twice is not a method, Bert.
      - barter_bert | Y156-10-12 | Twice is a very good record.
  - greta_sells | Y156-10-13 | I keep a copy of the board at the start of every year and compare it in the winter. It is the only way I know what actually moved. Last year I could not find the copy for Y150 and I have no idea where it went.
    - copperkate | Y156-10-13 | I have one for Y150. Come and see me and copy it.
      - greta_sells | Y156-10-13 | I will. I have been quoting Y150 from memory for a year and I do not trust the memory.
