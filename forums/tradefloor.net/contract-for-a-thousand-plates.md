# A contract for a thousand plates: what do I put on the paper?
board: Contracts   date: Y152-03-08   author: contract_ines   status: open

- contract_ines | Y152-03-08 | A buyer wants a thousand copper plates over four months. I have the price agreed and the paper in front of me and I want the board's opinion on what must be on it.
  - copperkate | Y152-03-08 | The delivery dates, the rate at which they will be accepted, and what happens if they are late. Not the price again. The price is the easy part.
    - contract_ines | Y152-03-08 | They want a single delivery at the end.
      - copperkate | Y152-03-08 | Then you are storing a thousand plates for four months and the paper should say who pays for the store.
  - barter_bert | Y152-03-09 | And the quality. A plate is a plate until somebody says it is not. Say what a plate is on the paper, in the same words they use.
    - contract_ines | Y152-03-09 | They have not defined it.
      - barter_bert | Y152-03-09 | Then you define it and make them agree. That is not being difficult, that is being a supplier.
  - old_ferrous | Y152-03-09 | Put a clause for the market moving. Four months is long enough for the posted rate to change and for one of you to feel foolish. A price that can be reopened at a date is better than a price that ruins a friendship.
  - margin_mo | Y152-03-10 | And put the fee for the waste on the paper. A thousand plates make no waste, but if they change the order to hardened plate it does, and the paper should say who pays.
    - contract_ines | Y152-03-10 | That is a good clause and I would not have thought of it.
  - contract_ines | Y152-08-19 | Signed and delivered. One late week, no argument, and a second order for the winter. The fee clause was never used and I am glad it was there.
