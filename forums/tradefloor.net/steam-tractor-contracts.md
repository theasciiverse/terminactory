# Steam tractor contracts: who is actually buying?
board: Contracts   date: Y153-02-20   author: contract_ines   status: open

- contract_ines | Y153-02-20 | A steam tractor posts at seven thousand five hundred. I have brokered two and both buyers paid above the rate and both insisted on a delivery date. If you are building one on the strength of the posted rate, read the second page of whatever you are signing.
  - copperkate | Y153-02-20 | The rate is the rate. Above it is a market and below it is a favour. I have no objection to either as long as it is written down.
    - contract_ines | Y153-02-20 | That is the most reasonable thing you have ever said about a price.
      - copperkate | Y153-02-20 | Do not get used to it.
  - foreman_hale | Y153-02-21 | The delivery date is the hard part. A tractor is an engine block, a frame, four hardened gears, a control panel and a generator. The control panel alone is two control boards, four lamps, two gauges and a steel sheet. If you promise a date before the panel line exists, you have promised a date you cannot keep.
    - contract_ines | Y153-02-21 | Then do not promise the date. Promise the line and give a date after the line is running.
      - foreman_hale | Y153-02-21 | That is what I did the second time.
  - silo_sam | Y153-02-22 | And how does it leave the yard? A tractor is not a crate. I have moved one and it took a flat cart, four men and a morning.
    - contract_ines | Y153-02-22 | That is a haulage line on the paper and a haulage charge on the invoice. If the buyer will not pay it, he can come and collect it.
      - silo_sam | Y153-02-22 | He will not come and collect it.
        - contract_ines | Y153-02-22 | Then he will pay the line.
  - freight_fen | Y153-02-22 | The north gate cannot take a tractor either. I have turned one away and I would rather not do it twice. Put the route on the paper with the date.
    - contract_ines | Y153-02-23 | It is on the paper. Thank you.

  - barter_bert | Y153-02-23 | I have a buyer who wants two. What is the rate for two?
    - copperkate | Y153-02-23 | Fifteen thousand and a conversation about the date.
      - barter_bert | Y153-02-23 | That is not a discount.
        - copperkate | Y153-02-23 | It is a discount of nothing and a conversation you need to have. Two tractors is two panel lines.
  - margin_mo | Y153-02-24 | A cost note. The generator alone posts at one thousand one hundred and the engine block at one thousand one hundred and fifty. The tractor's parts, at posted rates, come to well over half the tractor's price before you have paid for the assembler's time. Seven thousand five hundred is a thin margin on a big machine.
    - contract_ines | Y153-02-24 | Which is why the date is where the money is. A thin margin and a late delivery is a loss.
  - old_ferrous | Y153-02-25 | I sold girders for thirty years and I never once promised a date. I promised a girder. The date was the buyer's problem and he always found one.
    - contract_ines | Y153-02-25 | That is a business model and it is not mine.
      - old_ferrous | Y153-02-25 | It worked for thirty years.
  - contract_ines | Y155-09-02 | Two years on. Four tractors brokered, three on time, one late by a month and paid for with a penalty clause I wrote myself. The clause was the best work I did on that contract.
