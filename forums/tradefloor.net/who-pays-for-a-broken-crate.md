# Who pays for a crate that arrives broken?
board: Haulage   date: Y149-08-19   author: freight_fen   status: open

- freight_fen | Y149-08-19 | A crate arrived with a corner stoved in and four hundred ingots on the cart floor. The hauler says it was loaded badly. The loader says it was driven badly. I am the man in the middle with a broom.
  - silo_sam | Y149-08-19 | It was loaded badly if it moved. A crate that is strapped does not stove a corner on a straight road.
    - freight_fen | Y149-08-19 | The road is not straight.
      - silo_sam | Y149-08-19 | Then it was strapped badly and driven badly, and you will not get a straight answer from either of them.
  - cart_and_crate | Y149-08-20 | The honest answer is that the corner was probably weak before it left. Crates get used and a used crate has a weak corner. Nobody inspects a crate.
    - freight_fen | Y149-08-20 | That is the first answer that has not blamed a man.
      - cart_and_crate | Y149-08-20 | It blames a crate. Crates do not argue.
  - contract_ines | Y149-08-21 | Read the paper. If it says "loaded by the sender, carried at the carrier's risk" then the carrier pays and the argument is over. If it says nothing, you are paying for a lesson.
    - freight_fen | Y149-08-21 | It says nothing.
      - contract_ines | Y149-08-21 | Then split it, write the clause for next time, and do not let it become a feud.
  - browncoat_bex | Y149-08-22 | Crates are cheap and ingots are not. Write that on the clause.
